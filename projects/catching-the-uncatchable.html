Research Paper · 2026

Catching the Uncatchable: Predicting Abrupt Market Movements

Jump-Diffusion GJD Neural ODE Neural Networks Time Series Modeling Surge Prediction

Abstract

Abrupt price surges are rare but economically significant events that are difficult to forecast using conventional models. This work frames surge prediction as a binary classification problem and studies it from two complementary angles: a stochastic modeling perspective based on first-passage-time theory, and a neural approach built on Neural ODEs.

On the stochastic side, we introduce a generalized jump-diffusion (GJD) process with correlated jump components, relaxing the standard independence assumption in classical jump-diffusion models. On the neural side, we adapt NODE from trajectory reconstruction to classification and compare it against LSTM, CNN, and Transformer baselines. Experiments on KOSDAQ, IWM, and S&P 500 suggest that NODE consistently outperforms both deep learning baselines and SDE-based benchmarks for predicting sharp upward price movements in high-volatility settings.

Task
Binary surge classification
Surge threshold
≥ 20% price increase
Input window
30-day lookback
Markets
KOSDAQ, IWM, S&P 500

Problem

Financial markets frequently experience sudden discontinuous price movements driven by macro shocks, liquidity events, or sector-wide sentiment shifts. These jumps are often correlated across assets within short time windows, yet most classical jump-diffusion formulations treat jump sizes as independent.

At the same time, purely parametric stochastic models may be too rigid for noisy real-world return series, while flexible neural sequence models can overfit spurious patterns. The central question is whether continuous-time neural dynamics can improve surge prediction without sacrificing the structure implied by financial time series.

Approach

We pursue two parallel lines of work and compare them on the same prediction task.

1. Generalized Jump-Diffusion (GJD)

Motivated by first-passage-time problems, we extend the classical Kou jump-diffusion framework by introducing correlation among jump components through a shared systematic factor. This GJD formulation is designed to capture the empirical observation that jumps occurring in close succession are often linked to common market-wide shocks.

  • Benchmark against Brownian motion, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, and standard Kou JD
  • Analyze threshold-crossing behavior via first-passage-time distributions
  • Separate diffusion and jump components using a boundary adjustment (BA) rule on log-returns

2. NODE-Based Classifier

We adapt Neural ODEs to surge classification rather than trajectory reconstruction. The model uses an LSTM encoder to extract sequential features, passes them through a NODE refinement layer that evolves latent states in continuous time, and outputs a surge probability through a classification head.

  • Architecture: LSTM encoder → NODE layer → classification head
  • Trained with a classification objective (not reconstruction)
  • Solved with a Dormand-Prince ODE solver and adjoint sensitivity for efficient backpropagation
  • Compared against LSTM, CNN, and Transformer direct-prediction baselines
  • NODE-T variant included as a controlled comparison trained for trajectory reconstruction

Features. All neural models use a 30-day window of four standardized inputs: price, volume, Sharpe ratio, and volatility. Models are pooled across tickers within each market during training.

Experimental Setup

We evaluate on three markets chosen for geographic and structural diversity:

  • KOSDAQ (Korea Exchange, 2017 to 2024): a growth-oriented market with frequent sharp moves in individual names
  • IWM (iShares Russell 2000 ETF, 2021 to 2026): a U.S. small-cap proxy for broader generalization testing
  • S&P 500 (2016 to 2024): a large-cap U.S. benchmark used in Appendix E for robustness across lower-noise environments

KOSDAQ and IWM serve as the primary high-volatility testbeds in the main analysis. S&P 500 is included to test whether conclusions transfer to a more stable market, and to compare performance across varying look-back windows and prediction horizons (Appendix E). Additional stochastic benchmarks (OU, GBM) and extended robustness tables are also reported in the appendix.

A positive label is assigned when the predicted surge probability exceeds a decision threshold of 0.5 and the realized forward price change reaches at least 20%. Stochastic models (JD and GJD) are calibrated separately per ticker, while neural models are trained in a pooled fashion across all tickers in each market.

Key Findings

Preliminary results from the paper (placeholder summary):

  • Neural network models consistently outperform classical stochastic benchmarks on high-volatility KOSDAQ and IWM
  • Among stochastic models, GJD improves over standard JD by allowing correlated jumps, but remains below neural performance in high-noise settings
  • NODE achieves the strongest overall results among all tested architectures on KOSDAQ and IWM, suggesting that continuous-time latent dynamics help suppress spurious features
  • IWM experiments largely confirm the patterns observed on KOSDAQ, with improved recall across neural models
  • On S&P 500 (Appendix E), the performance gap narrows: the lower-noise environment favors simpler models such as OU, while NODE remains competitive but less dominant than on small-cap datasets

Future Directions

Several extensions are natural next steps: hybrid neural-SDE models (e.g., neural SDEs) that combine parametric stochastic structure with flexible function approximation, cross-market transfer learning, and richer feature sets beyond the four baseline inputs.
