Research Paper · 2026

Predicting Price Surges with Neural ODE

A classification-oriented continuous-time framework for predicting rare and abrupt price movements in noisy financial markets.

Neural ODE Rare-Event Classification Financial Time Series Generalized Jump-Diffusion First-Passage Time Low-SNR Learning

Abstract

Predicting abrupt price surges in financial time series is challenging because such events are rare, market data have low signal-to-noise ratios, and temporal relationships can be difficult to distinguish from random fluctuations.

This work formulates price-surge prediction as a binary classification problem and adapts a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (NODE) framework, originally developed for continuous-path reconstruction, to direct classification. The model combines an LSTM encoder, a continuous-time NODE refinement layer, and a classification head. The NODE layer constrains the evolution of the latent representation and helps reduce overfitting to noisy financial patterns.

We compare the proposed NODE classifier with LSTM, CNN, and Transformer baselines, as well as classical stochastic models. To strengthen the stochastic comparison, we introduce a generalized jump-diffusion (GJD) process that allows jump sizes to share a systematic component. Experiments on Korean and U.S. equity markets show that the classification-oriented NODE consistently outperforms both neural and stochastic benchmarks.

Research Question

Financial time series are characterized by substantial noise, weak predictive signals, and occasional extreme movements. Standard neural sequence models can capture complex temporal relationships, but their high flexibility also makes them susceptible to fitting spurious fluctuations rather than learning stable predictive patterns.

Classical stochastic models provide greater structure and interpretability, but their fixed parametric assumptions may be too restrictive for complex real-world data. The central question of this work is whether the continuous-time structure and implicit regularization of NODE can improve rare-event classification in noisy financial markets.

Methodology

The study centers on a classification-oriented NODE model and evaluates it against neural and stochastic benchmarks under a common price-surge prediction task.

1. Classification-Oriented NODE

The proposed model consists of an LSTM encoder, a NODE refinement layer, and a feed-forward classification head. The LSTM first compresses the observed feature sequence into a latent initial condition. This representation is then evolved through a learned continuous-time dynamical system before being used to estimate the probability of a future price surge.

Unlike NODE models designed to reconstruct complete future trajectories, the proposed model is trained directly for binary classification. This removes unnecessary trajectory-reconstruction objectives and focuses the learned representation on information relevant to the surge event.

  • Architecture: LSTM encoder → NODE refinement layer → classification head
  • Directly optimized for price-surge classification
  • Continuous latent dynamics integrated from t = 0 to t = 1
  • Dormand–Prince adaptive ODE solver
  • Adjoint-state method for backpropagation through the ODE solver
  • Cross-entropy loss with L1 regularization

2. Neural Benchmark Models

The NODE classifier is compared with three direct-prediction architectures: LSTM, CNN, and Transformer. Each model receives the same input information and is trained under the same binary classification setting.

  • LSTM: uses the same encoder and classification head as NODE but removes the intermediate ODE layer
  • CNN: captures local temporal patterns through one-dimensional convolutional layers
  • Transformer: models relationships across the input sequence through self-attention

Because the LSTM baseline differs from NODE primarily through the absence of the ODE refinement layer, the comparison helps isolate the contribution of continuous-time latent dynamics.

3. Trajectory-Reconstruction Comparison

We also construct a trajectory-reproducing variant, denoted NODE-T. It uses the same encoder and NODE structure but replaces the classification head with a decoder trained to reconstruct future feature trajectories.

At inference, the reconstructed price path is converted into a surge score. Comparing NODE with NODE-T helps distinguish the contribution of the direct classification objective from the contribution of the NODE architecture itself.

4. Generalized Jump-Diffusion Benchmark

To provide a structured stochastic benchmark, we extend the classical jump-diffusion framework by allowing jump sizes to share a common systematic component. In the resulting generalized jump-diffusion model, each jump is represented as the combination of a shared component and an idiosyncratic component.

This formulation relaxes the standard assumption that jump sizes are independent and identically distributed. It is intended to capture the possibility that consecutive market movements may be influenced by the same information shock or underlying event.

  • Introduces dependence among jumps through a shared systematic component
  • Separates ordinary fluctuations from jumps using a boundary-adjustment rule
  • Studies threshold crossing through first-passage-time analysis
  • Derives a tractable approximation for the Laplace transform of the first-passage-time distribution
  • Converts the recovered distribution into a model-implied surge probability

Input features. The neural models use a 30-day sequence containing four features: log-price return, log-trading-volume change, Sharpe ratio, and volatility. The Sharpe ratio and volatility are standardized using statistics computed from the training data.

Experimental Setup

The main experiments use individual equities from KOSDAQ and IWM. These markets provide high-volatility settings in which rare price movements and noisy temporal patterns occur relatively frequently.

  • KOSDAQ: Korean equities observed from 2017 to 2024
  • IWM: U.S. small-cap equities represented through the Russell 2000 universe, observed from 2021 to 2026
  • S&P 500 and additional U.S. datasets: used in supplementary experiments to evaluate performance under different market conditions, look-back windows, and prediction horizons

For each ticker and date, a positive label is assigned when the stock price increases by at least 20% at some point during the following 20 trading days. The prediction is based on information from the preceding 30 trading days.

Each ticker is divided chronologically into training, validation, and test periods using a 60%–10%–30% split. The data are not shuffled, preserving the temporal ordering and preventing future information from entering the training process.

Neural models are trained by pooling observations across tickers within each market. In contrast, the stochastic models are estimated separately for each individual ticker.

Evaluation

Performance is evaluated using precision, recall, and average precision (AP). Because surge observations are highly imbalanced, AP is used as the primary metric because it summarizes the precision–recall tradeoff across different classification thresholds.

Key Findings

  • NODE achieves the highest average precision among all evaluated neural and stochastic models on both KOSDAQ and IWM.
  • On KOSDAQ, NODE achieves an AP of 0.1830, compared with 0.1360 for the structurally comparable LSTM baseline.
  • The KOSDAQ improvement corresponds to an AP increase of approximately 35% over LSTM and is driven primarily by improved precision while maintaining comparable recall.
  • On IWM, NODE achieves an AP of 0.3135 and also substantially improves recall relative to the other neural models.
  • NODE produces smaller train–test AP gaps than LSTM on both KOSDAQ and IWM, providing empirical evidence that the NODE refinement layer improves generalization in noisy settings.
  • NODE outperforms NODE-T, indicating that its performance advantage is not explained solely by the continuous-time architecture. Directly optimizing the classification objective also contributes to the improvement.
  • GJD improves upon standard JD by incorporating dependence among jumps, making it the strongest stochastic benchmark in the main experiments.
  • Despite this improvement, the fixed parametric stochastic models remain substantially below the neural models in the high-volatility KOSDAQ and IWM settings.

Interpretation. The results suggest that the NODE layer functions as a constrained latent-space refinement mechanism. Its continuous-time dynamics help suppress noisy or spurious encoded features while preserving information that is useful for predicting rare price surges.

Market-Regime Dependence

The advantage of NODE is most pronounced in volatile and noisy environments such as KOSDAQ and IWM. In relatively stable markets such as the S&P 500, all models perform relatively poorly, although NODE continues to achieve the best results among the evaluated methods.

This pattern suggests that the value of continuous-time latent regularization depends on the underlying market regime. NODE is particularly useful when the model must extract weak predictive signals from substantial idiosyncratic noise.

Limitations and Future Directions

A primary limitation of NODE is computational cost. The adaptive ODE solver and adjoint-state gradient calculation require more computation than standard sequence models such as LSTM. The framework is therefore better suited to lower-frequency applications, including daily prediction, than to latency-sensitive high-frequency trading.

A promising extension is to combine neural architectures with stochastic differential equations. Neural SDE models could preserve the flexible function approximation of neural networks while explicitly incorporating stochastic market dynamics. Such hybrid models may be especially useful in lower-noise markets, where capturing smaller stochastic variations may be more important than filtering extreme noise.
